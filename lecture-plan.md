# Lecture Plan — "AI Won't Take Your Job. Someone Using AI Might."

**Thu Dec 3 · 9:30–10:45 · W.C. Morris, UCM · 75 min**
Audience: Psychology, Nursing, CS, Biology, Agriculture students

**Goal:** students walk out excited, with one concrete way to use AI in their field, and having asked real questions.

**Rule of thumb:** no more than 10 minutes without them doing or saying something.

## 1. Hook + Intro (9:30, 5 min)

- Open with a hands-up poll:
  - "Who used AI this week?"
  - "Who's used it for something *other* than homework?"
  - "Who's worried it'll affect your job?"
- **Story:** Oracle (Fortune 100) → Hy-Vee, a grocery company, and made *more* money.
  - Point: tech skills are valuable in every industry, including healthcare, farms, labs, clinics and retail. Every company is becoming a tech company.
- If there are survey results, show 1–2 numbers here ("X% of you said you're worried about your job. Let's talk about that.").

## 2. Four Myths (9:45, 10 min)

Put each myth on a slide and have the room vote true or false before you answer it.

| Myth | Reality |
|---|---|
| "AI is going to take my job." | AI takes over *tasks*, not whole jobs. People who know how to direct AI will replace people who don't. |
| "Using AI is cheating." | It depends on whether it replaces your thinking or speeds it up. Employers *expect* you to use it. |
| "It's just a Google you talk to." | That's the smallest thing it does. It can also draft, critique, analyze data, tutor, role-play and take actions. |
| "Companies need to build their own AI to be safe." | Very few do. Most use existing models, connect them to their own data, and add guardrails. That integration skill is what's in demand. |

**What employers want:** AI that improves **efficiency**, reduces **errors**, and increases **productivity** and **creativity**.

## 3. What an LLM Actually Is (9:45, 8 min)

Keep it non-technical:
- It's trained on a huge amount of text and learns to predict "what comes next" very, very well.
- **Good at:** language, summarizing, explaining, drafting, brainstorming, code, pattern-spotting.
- **Weak at:** knowing what it doesn't know. It can be confidently wrong ("hallucination").
- **The key takeaway:** *you* are the expert and the editor. AI is a fast, tireless intern. It's not an oracle.

**Demo (2 min):** Ask it something niche about UCM or Warrensburg so it gets a detail wrong. Then ask, "How confident are you? What would you need to verify?" This shows why you check its work.

## 4. AI in Your Field: Live Demos (9:53, 22 min)

Ask: "Who's here from ___?" and demo for the fields in the room, about 4 min each. For each one, show a **weak prompt first, then a strong prompt**.

### Nursing
- **Demo:** Paste a (fake) discharge instruction. Prompt: *"Rewrite this for a patient with a 6th-grade reading level who is anxious. Then list 3 questions they're likely to ask."*
- **Also:** NCLEX-style practice questions with explanations of why the wrong answers are wrong.
- **Guardrail:** Never enter real patient info (HIPAA). Always verify doses and clinical facts.

### Psychology
- **Demo:** *"Here are 5 anonymized interview responses about study stress. Identify themes, quote supporting lines, and tell me where you're uncertain."*
- **Also:** Draft survey items and check them for leading or biased wording. Explain a stats test in plain language.
- **Discussion hook:** "Should AI be used as a therapist?" This is a great debate topic.

### Computer Science
- **Demo:** Paste buggy code. Prompt: *"Find the bug, explain it like I'm a sophomore, then write a test that would have caught it."*
- **Also:** AI coding agents that build features end to end.
- **The shift:** The job is moving from *typing code* to *designing, directing and reviewing* it.

### Biology
- **Demo:** Paste a paper abstract. Prompt: *"Explain this to a first-year student, list the key assumptions, and propose a follow-up experiment."*
- **Also:** Write Python to analyze lab data.
- **Wow fact:** AlphaFold, an AI that predicts protein structures, won a share of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

### Agriculture
- **Demo:** Upload a photo of a diseased leaf. Prompt: *"What might this be? What would you check to confirm it? What are the treatment options and costs?"*
- **Also:** Precision ag (for example, sprayers that use computer vision to target weeds only), yield and weather planning, and drafting grant or loan applications.
- **Hook:** Farming is one of the most data-heavy jobs out there.

**Backup:** Have screenshots of every demo in the deck in case the Wi-Fi fails.

## 5. Beyond "A Google You Talk To" (10:15, 8 min)

Show the levels of AI use:
1. **Ask:** a question, like a search engine. *Most people stop here.*
2. **Collaborate:** give it context and a role, then iterate ("act as my critic," "ask me questions first").
3. **Create:** build things with it, like documents, analyses, code and study guides.
4. **Delegate:** agents that take actions across tools, like email, calendars, files and code.

**A prompt formula they can screenshot:**
> **Role** + **Context** + **Task** + **Format** + **"Ask me questions before you start."**

**Demo:** The same task done two ways, with a one-line prompt and then with the formula. Let the room judge which output is better.

**Tie-in for professors (brief):** BrightSpace course building, plus Jamie Copeland's tool for teaching AI by field. AI can take the routine work off professors' plates so they can focus on teaching. *(Confirm tool details with Jamie first.)*

## 6. Learning vs. Cheating (10:23, 5 min)

- **The test:** "Did AI replace your thinking, or make it better?"
- **Good uses:** a tutor that quizzes you, a critic of your draft, explaining a concept five different ways.
- **Bad uses:** submitting output you can't explain.
- **Why it matters:** In an interview or at a patient's bedside there's no copy-paste. The skill has to be in *your* head.
- Always follow each instructor's AI policy, and disclose when you use AI.

## 7. Q&A (10:28, 14 min)

How to get questions out of a quiet room:
- **Seed it:** "The question I get most is..." then answer it.
- **Turn and talk (1 min):** "Turn to your neighbor. What's one thing you'd want AI to do in your future job?" Then call on 2–3 pairs.
- **Anonymous:** a QR code to a form or Slido, so shy students can ask.
- **Stump the AI:** Take a student's question and ask the AI live.
- **Give a reward:** a small prize or candy for the first question.

Seed questions to have ready:
- Will AI replace nurses, therapists or programmers?
- Which AI tool should I use?
- Is it okay to put my notes or data into AI? What about privacy?
- How do I put AI skills on my résumé?
- Can AI be biased? *(Psychology and Nursing students will care about this.)*

## 8. Close (10:42, 3 min)

- **One action this week:** Pick one task from your major and do it with AI using the prompt formula.
- Repeat the theme: "AI won't take your job. Someone who knows how to use it might. Be that person."
- Show contact info or LinkedIn, plus a QR code to the post-survey.
